The [[Founder agreement]] is an agreement between the founders of a company on how ownership is shared between them.

The [[#Splitting ownership|ownership has to be split]] before the company can reach out to potential investors.
# Content
The [[Founder agreement]] adressess five main issues:
1. Who are the founders
2. Salaries and other compensations
3. Obligations and rights between company and founders
4. Ownership allocation
5. Contingencies impacting rights of the founders (stronger or weaker)
# Splitting ownership
Two approaches exist:
- Egalitarian approach: an equal split between founders
- In-equal approach: rewarding different contributions with different amounts of ownerhsip
## In-equal approach
To determine who gets more or less ownership, two views can be used:
- **Backward-looking**: objectively take stock of who contributed what (idea, funding, etc.)
- **Forward-looking**: think about who will have to contribute what as the company develops (think about incentives)

A paper by Bengt Holmström (Nobel, 2016)[^1] emphasizes the importance of incentives. It should impact the share allocation and should be balanced such that everyone stays on board.

The [[FAST tool]] offers a way to allocate founder shares.

[^1]: Holmström, B. R. (1978). _On Incentives and Control in Organizations_. Stanford University.
