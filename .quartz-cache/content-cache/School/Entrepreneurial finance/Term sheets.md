Term sheets are a set of contractual clauses that govern the rights and obligations of both the [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneurs]] and investors.

In order to accommodate for the uncertainty that entrepreneurship brings, [[Contingent contracting]] is practiced.

A TS (term sheet) handles different important subjects:
1. [[#Cash flow rights]]
2. [[#Compensation and employment]]
3. [[#Control rights]]
4. [[#Investor liquidity]]
5. Other clauses
# Cash flow rights
Cash flow rights in [[Term sheets]] do two things:
- Incentivize parties for high performance
- Give investors *downside protection*
## Convertible stocks
Investors obtain [[Convertible stock]]s, which allows them to choose between:
- a debt-like payoff in case of failure
- Common equity in case of success
## Participating preferred stock
Investors can also combine debt and equity features using [[Participating preferred stock]]s, which also returns them a share of the exit value.
## Preference for preferred stock 
Three reasons why preferred stocks are better:
1. Provide incentives to the founders
2. Screens out weaker projects
3. Align investors' and entrepreneurs' expectations
# Compensation and employment
When the venture is funded, the founders become employees. This implies that they:
- can be fired.
- are subject to [[Non-compete agreement|NCA]]/laws.
- They confer their [[Intellectual property rights|Intellectual property]] to their company. 
## Vesting
Most compensation for the founders and early employees comes from future sales of common stock. Using [[Vesting]], founders are subject to two vesting dimensions:
- Time vesting (linear milestones, etc.)
- Performance vesting (accelerated when reaching and IPO, acquisition, etc.)

Stock options for the founders and early employees come from the [[Stock option pool]] of the firm.
# Control rights
A company's charter and by-laws introduce four types of control rights:
1. Shareholders' voting rights
2. [[Board of Directors]]
3. Contractual agreements
4. Informal control
