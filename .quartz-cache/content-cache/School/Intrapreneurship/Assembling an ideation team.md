# Multidisciplinary team
Research has shown that multidisciplinary teams tend to fail more, but have better idea potential. The ideas that don't fail are often of higher quality.

![[Multidisciplinary teams and value of innovation.png]]

For big innovations, a multidisciplinary team is a must. However, if the innovations required are incremental, a single discipline team might be better as they fail less frequently.
# Ideation process
The [[Design thinking]] methodology can be used to generate good ideas.

[[CREAX]] employs [[Design thinking]] in a three step process:
1. Characterizing the problem
2. Brainstorming and selecting new ideas
3. Implementing solutions
## Characterizing the problem
The team starts of with characterizing the product or service to gain a good understanding of it. They pay attention to four pivotal aspects:
1. Performance
2. Harm / durability
3. Convenience
4. Cost

This can be used as a basis to generate new solutions in the next step.
## Brainstorming and selecting ideas
### Brainstorming
The team then starts with brainstorming for ideas. Brainstorming sessions are optimally 60 to 90 minutes.

They begin with defining the *product DNA*, which consists of all the properties of the product on one side and the functions of each property on the other side.

Then, they can practice:
- **Variation seeking**: look at the properties one by one and think about what would happen if they changed the property, does it have a good or bad impact?
- **Analogy seeking**: come up with other technologies (from other industries) to execute each function. 

Additionally, the team can rely on:
- Observation of user
- User surveys
- A sketch of the [[Customer journey]]
### Selecting
Now, the team has to select the best ideas. They have to go for divergence during brainstorming to convergence in selection.
## Implementing solutions
Finally, the solutions from the previous step have to be implemented into the customer's product. 

A popular method for this is [[Agile development]].
