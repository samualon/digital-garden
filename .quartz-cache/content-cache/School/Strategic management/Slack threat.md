Slack represents the sub-optimization of the firms [[Competitive advantage]], which results in a decrease of [[Value-capturing]] and dissipating of the [[Economic value added|Added economic value]]. The firm doesn't exploit the full potential of its [[Competitive advantage]].

The reverse of slack is [[Organizational effectiveness]].
# Mitigation
Seven barriers for slack exist:
1. Generating information
2. Monitoring behavior
3. Offering performance incentives
4. Shaping norms
5. Bonding resources
6. Changing governance
7. Mobilizing for change
## Generating information
In order to identify and fix slack, information on the current level of slack is necessary. One way is to benchmark against competitors and see where slack lies.
## Monitoring behavior
By more closely monitoring behavior, *inappropriate* behavior can be stopped early. Furthermore, the posibility of detection (and potentially a punishment) can decrease slack tendencies.

## Shaping norms
By cultivating a culture around norms, values and a mission, moral suasion[^1] can decrease slack.
## Bonding resources
Because of the [[Agency cost of free cash flow]], a firm can reduce slack by piling up debt and this way reducing the size of [[Cash flow]]s.
## Changing governance
By having a strong board, insiders that dominate can be stopped.
## Mobilizing for change
Forcing change at the top can often be necessary to reduce slack.

[^1]: Moral suasion is an appeal to morality, in order to influence or change behavior
