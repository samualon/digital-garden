A corporate strategy is the act of creating a [[Competitive advantage]] by combining all the parts, or business units together. Companies with a good corporate strategy add more to their [[Competitive advantage]] as a whole, than the sum of all individual business units.

Through a strong [[Corporate strategy]], the individual [[Strategic Business Unit|SBU]] gain a [[Competitive advantage]] through its corporate affiliation.
# Creating a corporate strategy
To build a corporate strategy, two steps are necessary:
1. Build a portfolio of businesses and define the model for [[Corporate advantage]].
2. Design the group and organize for [[Corporate advantage]].
## Build a portfolio
Finding the right businesses for the portfolio can be done using different frameworks:
- [[BCG Growth share matrix]]
- [[McKinsey Industry attractiveness-business strength matrix]]

### Planning for growth
Using the [[McKinsey Three horizons model]], the company can plan out what it needs in the short- and medium-run for growth in the long-run
### Value-based portfolio management
Two tests can be used to assess whether a planned expansion of company scope should be executed and how it should be executed. These tests are the [[Better off test]] and the [[Best alternative test]] (see [[CR14 Strategy and the Business Landscape Ch 06 Choosing Corporate Scope|CR14]]).

Value can be added either horizontally (people, strategy, targets, policies, relationships, technology, expertise, brand, etc.) or vertically ([[Cross-selling]], [[Economies of scale]], [[Economies of scope]], shared resources, etc.) (see [[CR14 Strategy and the Business Landscape Ch 06 Choosing Corporate Scope|CR14]]).
## Design and organize
When the company knows which businesses to incorporate, it has to think about the organization of the corporation as a whole.
### Resource alignement
On the [[The resource continuum.png|Resource continuum]], all aspects should be aligned.
### Building blocks
Building blocks can be used to create a company under a *superstructure*. 

![[Building blocks for a corporation.png]]

These building blocks can be devised based on different things they can all be based on:
- Products
- Customers
- Functions
- Geographies

A matrix organization is an organization where multiple of these types of building blocks are combined (f.e. products and customers).
### Decentralization and synergy
A trade-off between decentralization and [[Synergy]] exists. Little decentralization results in more [[Synergy]] and vise versa.
