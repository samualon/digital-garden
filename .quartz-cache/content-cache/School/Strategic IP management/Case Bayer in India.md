Bayer is a major chemical and pharma firm. The case concerns a drug that they developed against liver and kidney cancer.
# Trademarks
For a pharmaceutical company, patents are important for they drugs, but [[Trademark]]s are also pivotal. When patent protection runs out, generic companies can copy you. If you have a strong [[Trademark]], you might still keep your competitive position.
# High prices
Parma companies are known to charge high prices for drugs, because:
- incorporate expensive R&D spending
- rare diseases give lower incentive of developing the drug

But from a welfare view, you want people in need to receive the drugs they need.
# Nexavar
Bayer asked 5500 EUR per month in India for their drug Nexavar (same as in the US). Compared to the GDP per capita, this is an extremely high price (41 times GDP/capita).

For this reason, the India state set a compulsory license for the drug.

Compulsory licensing is allowed conform [[Trade-related aspects on IP rights|TRIPS]] when:
- its pharma
- there's a national health hazard
- it's three years after the patent was granted
- they only sell it locally
- pay a royalty to the licensor
## Justification
1. Bayer is not supplying the drug widely enough (not in rural areas).
2. Price is too high compared to GDP/capita
3. In India, the drug has to be produced locally. Bayer didn't produce it there.
## Consequences for Bayer
- Lost incomes in India
- Lost incomes due to illegal export
- Other countries can follow
