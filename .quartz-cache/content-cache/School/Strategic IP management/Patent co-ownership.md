[[Patent co-ownership]] is often employed.
# Legal context
Two forms of [[Patent co-ownership]] exist, for Europe and the US.
## Europe
A European co-patent allows a firm to exploit it freely, but it can't give out licenses or assign interests without permission of the second party.
## US
A US co-patent allows a firm to exploit it freely and license and sell it without the permission of the second party.
# Co-ownership problems
Good co-ownership contracts should be in place to evade problems.

Two contract clauses are suggested:
1. Rule out unilateral licensing[^1] with competitors.
2. Separate exploitation paths (easy in university-firm context, but harder in companies within a similar industry).
# Research
Research has shown that its best for firms to firstly try to single-own patents when possible. They should only co-own if its the only way to develop the technology.

[^1]: Only one licensor and one licensee
