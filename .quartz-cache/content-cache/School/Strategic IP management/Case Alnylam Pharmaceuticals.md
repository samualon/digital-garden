RNAi is RNA interference, which is a technology that allows to make drugs against diseases caused by genetics. RNAi could interfere the process of DNA $\rightarrow$ RNA $\rightarrow$ Proteins by creating RNA strengs that bind to the RNA that would result in proteins that cause the disease.

This means that all diseases of which we understand the genetic causes, we can use RNAi to fix it.
# Patents
There were 8 - 10 patents that the technology is based on. Alnylam had created an IP portfolio to get access to all of these 8 building blocks for this technology.

To get acces to these patents, they licensed, [[Cross-licensing]] and acquire patents to practice the technology. Two patents were crucial:
1. Tuschl 1
2. Tuschl 2
## Tuschl 1
Patent resulting from experimenting on non-human cells. It showed that RNAi worked in non-human cells. The patent was owned by multiple research institutes as they developed it together:
- Max Planck institute
- MIT
- Whitehead

Alnylam had a non-exclusive license to the technology, since the patent was co-owned by the three insitutions, not all of them wanted exclusivity for Alnylam (Max Planck did). 

The patent was pending during the research.
## Tuschl 2
This patent described a slightly different way of creating the RNA strengs to be used in the RNAi, which didn't destroy human cells and thus could be used for human application.

Only Max Planck Institute owned the patent, as they only did the development. Alnylam had an exclusive license for this patent. 

This patent was already granted during the research.
## Other patents
Some complementary patents however were needed to develop the technology.
# Alnylam development
Alnylam had access to the needed patents, but needed money for the development of the technology. They license and sub-license these patents that they had to big pharma non-exclusively. This was possible since its a platform technology as it can be used for many different diseases. Different pharma firms used it for different diseases, so no interference.

They always included a [[Grant-back clause]]s in their [[IP licensing]] to these pharma companies.
## Weak link of the portfolio
The [[#Tuschl 1]] patent had multiple owners, and they disagreed on the scope of the patent. Max Planck claimed that the patent's scope is limited to non-human cells. MIT and Whitehead believe in a broader scope of the patent.
# Merck
Merck is a pharma company that wants to get access to the RNAi technology. How can Merck gain the right to develop and use this technology without infringement?
- Get a license
- They already have access on Tuschl 1 and wait on the outcome of the lawsuit on the scope of Tuschl 1 and hope that it is broader.
- [[Surrounding IP strategies]] of Alnylam such as patents on delivery methods (Making sure the RNA gets to the right place)
- Acquire Alnylam
- Try to invalidate patents

Merck didn't do any of these things, lost intrest and dropped the research. Alnylam then acquired their patents.
