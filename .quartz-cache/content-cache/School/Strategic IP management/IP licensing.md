Monetization through innovation can be done through two ways:
1. Developing and launching a product or service
2. Licensing
# Definition
IP licensing concerns two parties, the licensor and the licensee. The licensor offers permission to use a certain technology in exchange for license payments from the licensee.
# Trade-off
When licensing, a company generates revenues from license payments. On the other hand, by out-licensing, the licensee becomes stronger and could worsen the competitive position of the licensor in the long-run.
## Licensor learning
An oversight of the previous paragraph is the potential of learning by the licensor. Additionally to revenue generation, a licensor can learn through the interaction of the licensee.

# Components
A licensing deal consists of four components:
1. Scope 
2. Payments
3. Obligations of licensor and licensee
4. Duration and termination
## Scope
Multiple options:
- Exclusive or non-exclusive license?
- License reserves rights to application and geography or not?
- Sub-licensing allowed?
- [[Grant-back clause]]? 
## Payments
Either a fixed or variable fee is possible.

Fixed fee:
- Upfront payment
- Milestone payments (during development)

Variable fee:
- Percentage of sales
- Fixed amount per unit sold
## Obligations
### Of the licensee
An exclusive licensee can *sit* on the technology, not developing it. Development requirements can prevent this, such as:
- Development dates
- Minimum sales
- Milestone-based royalty fees
### Of the licensor
A licensor can be required to offer:
- information
- training services
- meetings
- warranties on validity of the technology
## Duration
Multiple options:
- Fixed term?
- Lifetime deal?
- When is early termination possible?
# Research
Research has shown the following:
- Larger firms are less inclined to out-license.
- Non-core technologies (not essential to the core of the business) are more likely to be out-licensed.
- Broad patents are more likely to be out-licensed.
