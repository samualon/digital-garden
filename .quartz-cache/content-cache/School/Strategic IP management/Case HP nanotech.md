HP has several technology groups that do research. Technologies resulting from these technology units are then incorporated into HP.

These technology groups frequently collaborate with universities and other research institutions to develop technologies.
# University collaboration
HP likes to work with universities because:
- access to talent (students)
- access to professors
- increase visibility (marketing and recruitment)
- access to technologies (of the universities)

Universities like to work with firms because:
- **access to funding** (public funding doesn't grow over time, so need private funding)
- access to data
- disseminate[^1]
- keep curricula relevant/up-to-date
# HP and CNSI
HP and CNSI took a lot of time to set up a collaboration because:
- many actors involved (and many had low rank in organization $\rightarrow$ have to act according to strict guidelines)
- different views on [[Foreground IP]] (who gets ownership and access):
	- HP wanted a non-exclusive but royalty-free license
	- CNSI were willing to license, but royalty-based, and exclusively (the administrators were used to exclusivity)

They solved this problem by bringing in people very high in the hierarchy. They found a consensus: distinction between projects that HP is directly involved $\rightarrow$ royalty-free license, and projects that HP is not directly involved $\rightarrow$ royalty-based license.

- Faster negotiations
