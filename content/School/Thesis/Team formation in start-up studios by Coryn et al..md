**Title**: Team formation in start-up studios: a qualitative research
**Author**: Samuel Coryn and Jakob Vanden Bussche
**Year**: 2024
**Research question**: How do start-up studios compose teams for each project?
# Abstract
The study researched how [[Start-up studio]]s form teams for each new start-up.

It interviewed seven different European start-up studios using semi-structured interviews and a six-step [[Thematic analysis]].
# Literature review
## Social networks
### Network models (Aldrich & Kim)
([[How social networks affect entrepreneurial team formation and search by Aldrich]])

The quality of the founders' networks has an impact on the efficiency of the recruitment.

Three main models for social networks exist:
1. [[Random networks]]
2. [[Small world networks]]
3. [[Scale free network]]

The [[Small world networks]] and [[Scale free network]] models both can apply to start-up studios:
- Close environment of founders can have impact on how easily they can find members.
- Founders can have ties with people that have a big network (hubs).
### Impact of networks (Brüderl)
It has been shown that [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneur]]s who can rely on their [[Strong-tie network]] can increase the succes of their start-ups. This supports the [[Small world networks]] model from [[#Network models]].
## Team member selection (Lazar)
([[Entrepreneurial team formation by Lazar]])

Two main strategies exist for the recruitment of team members:
- [[Resource-seeking team formation]]
- [[Interpersonal attraction team formation]]

Founders can combine the two to create hybrid team formation strategies.
## Entrepreneurial team formation (Klada)
([[Unraveling Entrepreneurial Team Formation by Klada]])

Four crucial aspects of team formation exist:
1. Candidate pools
2. Information sources
3. Selection criteria
4. Cooperation patterns
### Candidate pools
Because new founders might not have a successful track record yet, many potential members might not be interested, decreasing candidate pools. This forces them to rely on [[Strong-tie network]]s.
### Information sources
Since recruitment is a skill of its own, new [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneur]]s might not possess this. Again, this forces them to rely on their [[Strong-tie network]] for information on recommendations for potential members.
### Selection criteria
Often, [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneur]]s will prioritize soft skills over hard skills in order to make resilient teams.
### Cooperation patterns
Four typical patterns for cooperation are found under the financial restrictions of a [[Startup|Start-up]]:
- Offering decision-making power (equity)
- Offering future prospects in the company
- Reducing labor costs through half-time employment
- Eliminating labor costs through internships with fresh graduates
## Entrepreneurial failure (Lattacher & Wdowiak)
Having had failed entrepreneurial experience can have a positive impact on their future success, but the perception of the learning is pivotal. They require two things:
1. Emotional resilience (to draw correct conclusions)
2. Creative skills (to apply the learned things to new contexts)
## Entrepreneurial mindset (Salisu)
Long-term entrepreneurial success requires *Grit*, or the perseverance and passion for long-term goals. *Grit* has two dimensions:
- Perseverance of effort
- Consistency of interest

Both are positively correlated to career succes, where resilience is the crucial link.
# Methodology
This is a qualitative study which employs a [[Thematic analysis]].
## Participants
Properties of the participants:
- From employees to high-level management (founders)
- Small and large studios
- Young and old studios
- European
## Data collection
Three categories of questions:
1. Team formation process
2. Team member characteristics
3. Founders' networks
## Data analysis
The interviews were transcribed and analyzed using the [[Six phase method of thematic analysis by Braun and Clarke]].
## Limitations
- Limited sample might not represent population
- [[Response bias]]
- [[Researcher bias]]
- Geographical constraint (Europe)

## Key team elements
The team formation process starts with the recruitment of top-level management:

| Amount | Desc                                                    |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 6      | Recruitment of CEO, CTO or both                         |
| 1      | Recruitment of CEO, CTO, CFO and CMO                    |
| 3      | Studios active in the tech industry recruit CEO and CTO |
Three crucial aspects of members exist:
1. Background and experience
2. Soft and hard skills
3. Personality
### Background and experience
| Amount | Desc                                                                           |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 5      | Requirment of prior entrepreneurial experience (zero to one)                   |
| 4      | Search for members that started 2 or more ventures                             |
| 1      | Searched for failed entrepreneurship                                           |
| 2      | Entrepreneurial experience important bcs broader network                       |
| 2      | B2B studios recruit members from B2B environment (experience)                  |
|        | Specific skills can be learned in-house                                        |
| 1      | Recruit from different industry $\rightarrow$ apply trends from other industry |
### Soft and hard skills
Opinions about the balance between soft and hard skills were mixed. Studios that prioritize soft skills did so because they believe these are essential to survive the turbulent entrepreneurial environment.
### Personality
Almost all studios pressed that a strong entrepreneurial mindset is required. Not only passion for entrepreneurship, but also the industry and project.
## Finding the needed profiles
[[Start-up studio|Start-up studios]] rely on four methods of reaching potential members:
1. Spontaneous applications
2. Network of core members (CEO, CTO)
3. Network of the studio
4. External engagement
### Spontaneous applications
Three studios relied on spontaneous applications, one of these only relied on these applications. These studios were larger and had a strong reputation. This method saves resources, no need to actively search.
### Network of core members
Four studios relied first on this network. It's favorable bcs the core members can better estimate who will fit in the team.
### Network of the studio
Three studios first relied on this network. The other four used these to fill the gaps left after relying on the core members' networks.

In conclusion, all studios rely on their personal networks to identify potential members.
### External engagement
Three studios attended entrepreneurial events to:
- Recruit members
- Increase publicity

This was often the last method to find members.

One studio cooperated with universities. One used recruitment agencies as a final resort.
