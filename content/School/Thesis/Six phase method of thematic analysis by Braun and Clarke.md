The six phase method by Braun and Clarke is a method for [[Thematic analysis]] for [[Qualitative data]].

It uses a sequence of six steps. These steps shouldn't be seen as purely linear, but more as a recursive process.
# The six steps
1. Familiarization with the data
2. Coding
3. Searching for themes
4. Reviewing themes
5. Defining and naming themes
6. Writing up
## Familiarization with the data
The researcher should read and re-read the data to fully immerse themselves in the data, while trying to already note initial analytical observations.
## Coding
The researcher should at pithy labels to important parts of the data, not only to reduce the data, but also to add a way to read the data more conceptually.

After this, the codes should be collated[^1].
## Searching for themes
Themes are meaningful patterns in the data, related to the research question.

The researcher has to actively find recurring themes in the codes and organize these codes together into different themes.
## Reviewing themes
The researcher tries to review the themes by assessing whether they tell a compelling and convincing story about the full data set. Additionally, he should think about how the different themes are related to each other.

It might be necessary to combine, split or scrap themes.
## Defining and naming themes
The researcher should carefully analyse every theme and define what each theme tells about the data, *what is the essence of the theme*?

This should result in a concise and informative name.
## Writing up
The researcher will finally use the different themes and extracts related to the themes to tell a compelling story about the data.

[^1]: Sorted and ordered.
