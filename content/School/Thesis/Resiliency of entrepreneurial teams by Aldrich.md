**Title**: Teams that work together, stay together: Resiliency of entrepreneurial teams
**Author**: [[Phillip H. Kim]] and [[Howard E. Aldrich]]
**Year**: 2006
**Research question**: What is the impact of team stability in reaching an operational status?
# Abstract
The researched measured team stability by assessing member addition, exit and replacement. It also tracked gender and race.

It was found that stable teams are slightly more likely to reach operational status.
# Content
## Introduction
*Homophily*, the act of choosing similar individuals as yourself to join your team, can create more social solidarity and trust inside of the team. This might result in a more stable team.

However, lack of diversity can result in lack of adoptability and redundancy in knowledge between members (see [[Interpersonal attraction team formation]]).
## Results
Two important implications:
1. Homophily dominates functional diversity
2. Homophily speeds up operational status
### Homophily dominates functional diversity
As said before, economically a diverse team makes sense as the knowledge base is wider.

In practice, similarity between members seems to drive team formation. Teams seem to trade off competency for increased similarity and thus trust.
### Homophily speeds up operational status
Homophily inhibits change (adoptability), which increases likelihood of reaching operational status.
