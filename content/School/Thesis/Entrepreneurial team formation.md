# Traditional view
Traditionally, academics described entrepreneurial team formation as a purely sequential input-process-output.
![[Standard model of entrepreneurial team formation.png]]
# Integrative model
In the paper [[Entrepreneurial team formation by Lazar]], this previous model is nuanced and zoomed in.

![[Integrative model of entrepreneurial team formation.png]]

This new model shows an interdependence between the formation strategy and the incipient[^1] outcomes of the team. It highlights that team formation is a dynamic process, where the founders can go back and forth between forming and having a team.

Additionally, two formation strategies are highlighted which are shaped by the context that the founders are in:
- Interpersonal attraction
- Resource-seeking

These two strategies can be combined and have an impact on team-
- characteristics
- processes
- performance
## Formation strategies
The two main formation strategies, interpersonal attraction (psychological and sociological view) and resource-seeking (economical view), can be combined to improve shortcomings of each strategy individually:
- [[Interpersonal attraction team formation]]
- [[Resource-seeking team formation]]
### Combination
These strategies can be combined either parallel, a **hybrid strategy**, or sequentially, a **shifting strategy**.

A shifting strategy often occurs due to member exits when early performance indicators show a bad team performance. Shifting offers a way for the team to adres deficiencies.
### Stability
#### Founder exit
Teams that are founded using mostly interpersonal attraction, are less likely to experience founder exit as the founders have a better connection with the team, unlike with a resource-seeking approach.
#### Reaching operational status
Using interpersonal attraction creates a more rigid team that inhibits change. This results increases the likelihood that the team reaches an operational status.
### In practice
In practice, team formation is driven more by similarity through interpersonal attraction, than resource-seeking. Founders are willing to trade off competency for increased similarity and thus trust (see [[Resiliency of entrepreneurial teams by Aldrich]]).
