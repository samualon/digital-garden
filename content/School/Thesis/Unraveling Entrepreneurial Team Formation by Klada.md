**Title**: Unraveling Entrepreneurial Team Formation: A Qualitative Study Among Funded Ventures
**Author**: [[Triantafyllia-Varvara Klada]]
**Year**: 2018
**Research question**: How do founders narrow the gap between their knowledge and the knowledge needed to materialize their idea?
# Abstract
The paper aims to research founders' behavior when attempting to narrow the game between their skills and the required skills for the business. 

The study has shown that entrepreneurs account for the lack of:
- Legitimacy
- Knowledge
- Financial resources

They do this by leveraging their:
- Social networks
- Available information resources
- Cooperation agreements
- Selecting profiles aligned with the challenges and goals of the business

By mitigating the risks mentioned above, founders give themselves rise to more options concerning the team formation process.
# Content
## Introduction
Startups are a way for economies to grow. Their starting point is an unexplored opportunity, but the scarcity of human, financial, physical and social capital can make it difficult for founders to materialize their idea. The human capital, team members, constitutes a [[Startup]]s most crucial asset.

Earlier research has used *Inputs-Mediator-Outputs* models, but Klada states that these models don't capture the full complexity of the subject. This study focuses on how the entrepreneurs behave during the team [[Entrepreneurial team formation]] process. It touches on:
- Selection, characteristics that entrepreneurs look for
- Alternative compensation for team members
- Impact of the choice of candidate pools on selection and compensation

## Literature review
A founder will need a team formation process when his knowledge and experience is inadequate to meet the venture needs. In this case he will have to undertake the following:
- Identify needs (which resources (human) do I lack?)
- Locate candidate pools that include these resources
- Set selection criteria
- Decide pecuniary[^1] and non-pecuniary rewards
### Identification
Entrepreneurs often have to reach potential members through unconventional ways, such as using their own social networks.

It was found that founders rarely add members with whom they share no social ties. Literature has often made the distinction between [[Strong-tie network]] and [[Weak-tie network]]. 

Strong ties can help mitigate lack of brand legitimacy, but also prevent them from exploring wider, more suitable pools.

Weak ties offer a wide range of contacts and greater diversity in information.
### Selection
Entrepreneurs can employ two strategies:
- [[Interpersonal attraction team formation]]
- [[Resource-seeking team formation]]

Having a common culture improves team stability and communication, but prevents creativity and might limit the knowledge base of the team.

High diversity improves creativity and openness to change, but makes communication more difficult and increases the chances of in-team conflicts.

Research has shown that the chosen prioritized strategy depends on the venture's life cycle stage. At the beginning, founders mostly search for highly committed members, even if they might not have the best and broadest skill set. Entrepreneurs tend to scan their environment for members that they trust and share a common value system with.

This thus hints at a relation between the selection criteria used by entrepreneurs and the pools they access for candidates.
### Compensation
Candidates can be reluctant to joining a startup with no track record because they would have to relinquish an existing career and family life.

These members' expectations on compensation can vary based on:
- Marital status
- Compensation at previous employer
- Level of one's achievements
## Findings
![[Entrepreneurial team formation behavior per stage.png]]
The interviews have found that entrepreneurs are aware of a possible missmatch between their skills and knowledge and the skills and knowledge needed to fulfill the project.

They seem to have access to limited pools as their ventures typically lack:
- Brand legitimacy
- Financial resources
- Limited knowledge on member selection

The findings concern four different decision areas of a [[Startup]]:
1. Candidate pools
2. Information sources
3. Selection criteria
4. Cooperation patterns
### Candidate pools
As stated before, [[Startup]]s have trouble attracting members because of a lack of brand legitimacy and track record.

Founders typically try to overcome this obstacle by relying on their own [[Strong-tie network]]. A smaller group of [[Startup]]s leverage other networks, such as skills-based events, to gain publicity and attract new members. Such events have shown to play a pivotal role in candidate attraction.
### Information sources
Founders try to find new members to lessen the gap between their knowledge and skills and the needed knowledge and skills. One problem is that attracting and recruiting new members for a [[Startup]] is a skill on its own, one which few founders possess from the start.

Initially, founders mostly relied on their acquaintances' recommendations to decide who to add to the team. Only some used entrepreneurial networks for this end.

Another approach that's sometimes used is a trial period, where members can test the collaboration for a period without having to commit to an agreement. This way, uncertainty from these members of the venture are partly mitigated.

Finally, it was found that founders gain confidence and skill in the recruitment of members after they've added their first team member. It appears that this is due to the fact that they are able to learn from their mistakes during the first hiring.
### Selection criteria
Founders typically pay attention to the same set of selection criteria, but prioritize them in a different order.

Two categories can be distinguished, criteria related to:

| Team effectiveness         | Knowledge and skill gaps |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Resilience                 | Job requirement skills   |
| Openness to change         | Hard skills              |
| Cooperation skills         |                          |
| Passion for the project    |                          |
| Modest salary expectations |                          |
It seems that founders tend to prioritize soft skills oriented towards creating a resilient team above having a strong fit between member skills and required skills.

This can be explained by the fact that it's harder to attract the specific profiles needed when the financial capability of the venture is weak. This is supported by the fact that when these companies grow to become more financially stable, the focus of founders' criteria shifts towards hard skills.
### Cooperation patterns
Due to financial constraints, founders had to rely on non-pecuniary forms of compensation to attract new members. Three kinds were highlighted:
- Compensation conditional on success of the venture
- University freshmen working without monetary compensation
- Part-time employment

Or:
- Offering decision-making power
- Offering future prospects
- Reducing labor costs
- Eliminating labor costs

Again, increases in financial resources of the ventures allowed founders to attract members more easily. They can then rely on full-time employees by offering attractive compensation. Reception of additional funding can thus be a trigger of changes in an entrepreneurial team.
