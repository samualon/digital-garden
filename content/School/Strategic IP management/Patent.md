Patents protect technical inventions solving technical problems.

It offers a right to prevent others from making, using, selling or importing infringing products **in the country that the patent was registered**.

This right lasts for 20 years from the filling date of the patent.

These rights can be sold or used in [[IP licensing]] deals.
# Conditions
Three conditions have to be met in order to be able to receive a patent for your invention:
1. Novelty (not part of public domain)
2. Inventive step (not obvious)
3. Useful (industrial application)

One should try to have the broadest possible scope of the patent, while still being novel.
## Novelty
An invention is considered novel if it is not part of the *state-of-the-art*. The latter means everything made available to the public before the filling date of the patent.

This implies that there can't be any public disclosure of an invention before the filling (not even to potential investors), as this would make it *state-of-the-art*.

## National patent application
This registration is for a patent in the home country. Each country's patent office has it's own rules and procedures.
## Regional or international patent application
This registration is for a patent in multiple countries. This usually introduces reduced costs, as the procedure concerns more countries.

An example is the [[European patent]].
# Infringement
Infringement entails the making, using, selling or offering that which falls under patents of someone else.

An infringement can lead to:
- Shutdown of operations
- Dawn raid[^1]
- Payment of damages
- Criminal sanctions (in some countries)
# Fiscal incentives
Some countries (including Belgium) provide special tax regimes to incentivise [[Innovation]] through patenting. 

Tax reductions then apply to incomes originating from a patent.
# Pharma
The pharmaceutical sector can receive patents with a duration of over 20 years. This is allowed since the development of pharmaceuticals often takes 15 years, which only leaves 5 years of protection.
