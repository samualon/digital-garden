# Introduction
Information on technology can be valuable for firms. A good source of information requires the following properties:
1. Up-to-date (patent filing always happens before press releases)
2. Complete
3. Correct
4. Detailed
5. Well-structured
6. Easily accessible

[[Patent database]]s comply with these requirements, and are thus a good source of valuable information on technology.
# Categories of information
Patent information can offer answers to four groups of questions:
1. What solutions exist for a certain technical problem?
2. Is my invention patentable?
3. Am I infringing someone's patent?
4. **What is happening in a certain technological field?**
## Solutions to technical problems
[[Patent database]]s offer ways to search for technologies using keywords. This way, firms can determine if certain technologies exist that they can use.

Additionally, the [[International Patent Classification]] number categorizes all patents into a hierarchy.
## Patentable
How to check if your technology is patentable:
1. Check patents relevant to your invention.
2. If your solution didn't seem obvious after reading relevant patents, then your invention should be non-obvious.
3. Check publications on the internet for *prior-art* on your invention.
4. Hand relevant patents and other information sources you used during these tests in with your patent filing to support your request.
## Infringement
[[Patent database]]s allow you to check on the legal status of patents. This way you can determine if the technology you want to use is still active or not.
## Strategic information
Using [[Patent mapping]], firms can find valuable technological information and industry trends. Using this information, they can develop strategies.
