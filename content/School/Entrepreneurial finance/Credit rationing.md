Credit rationing is the occurrence of firms that are willing to borrow at equilibrium intrest rates, but are unable to do so.
# Models
Two models for *rational* credit rationing exist:
- [[Adverse selection]]
- [[Moral hazard]]

In both cases, firms exist that want to borrow at equilibrium (or higher) intrest rates but get rejected by banks due to these two implications of [[Asymmetric information]].
# Market failure
Credit rationing isn't a market failure, but an implication of market failure. Following aspects of the market lead to market failure:
- [[Asymmetric information]]
- [[Adverse selection]]
- [[Moral hazard]]

[[Adverse selection]] and [[Moral hazard]] can lead to [[Market failure]] in case of [[Asymmetric information]].
