$$ \Downarrow $$
Financial plan answers the two [[Financial plan#Key questions|key questions]]
$$ \Downarrow $$
Business plan proposes a full venture

The financial projections are needed to complete the fundamentals of the starting business, such as the financial plan and [[Business plan]].
# Drawback
Financial projections are:
- Inaccurate 
- Quickly outdated
- Optimistic
# Components
Financial projections consist of three components:
- [[Income statement projection]]: [[Business model]] and profitability
- [[Balance sheet]]: size, asset structure, financing
- [[Cash flow statement]]

# Building projections
A five step process can be used to develop financial projections:
1. Defining a timeline
2. Estimating revenues
3. Estimating costs
4. Building a financial model
5. Formulating the financial plan
## Defining a timeline
Several things have to be defined:
- [[Milestone|Milestones]] (can be done using a [[Gant chart]])
- Time horizon (From 2 to 20 years, average 5 to 6 years)
- ==Account detail between milestones==
- Time intervals across the horizon
## Estimating revenues
In the simplest form revenue is:
$$ Revenue = Price * Quantity $$
The objective is to define price and quantity:
- Need to define what one unit of quantity is (one good? one hour service? one customer? one contract?)
- Need to estimate the price
- Need to estimate quantity
### Top-down and bottom-up
Two approaches exist to estimate the quantity sold:
- [[Top-down revenue projection]]
- [[Bottom-up revenue projection]]

These two approaches can be combined by defining the estimated market share as:
$$ S = \frac{C}{M} $$
Four cases exist for this market share $S$:

| Case         | Corresponds to                                                                                           |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $S<5\%$      | *Small share*<br>Market share might be defined too broadly<br>Bottom-up strategy may be too conservative |
| $5\%<S<25\%$ | *Normal share*<br>Market share might not be realistic                                                    |
| $S>50\%$     | *Aggressive share*<br>Is it realistic that you're the largest player?                                    |
| $S>100\%$    | *Impossible share*<br>Market doesn't support the growth strategy<br>Dreaming                             |

## Building a financial model
To build a financial model, three steps have to be undertaken:
1. Create the three financial accounts
2. Assess the accounts
3. Test the model
### Creating the three accounts
A financial model consists of three accounts:
- [[Income statement projection]]
- [[Balance sheet projection]]
- [[Cash flow statement projection]] 
### Assess the accounts
Using the accounts, we can assess the potential financial up-/downside by:
- using different scenarios of profitability and benchmarking against competition
- evaluating need for [[Working Capital]]
- identifying funding needs

Additionally, entrepreneurs should try to keep [[Cash flow]] positive.

Having positive [[Net income]] is not a guarantee for positive [[Cash flow]]s.

![[Net income, CF and CB over time.png]]
### Testing financial projections
Instead of only having one restrictive path of projections, ventures should consider multiple possible paths.

Three methods that can be used:
- [[Scenario analysis]]
- [[Sensitivity analysis]]
- **Simplification** of the financial plan to make it more manageable (by unit model, limited industry metrics, smaller time horizon)
## Formulating the financial plan
The [[Financial plan]] answers two [[Financial plan#Key questions|key questions]]:
- Financial attractiveness, answered by the [[#Creating the three accounts|the three accounts]].
- Needed financial resources, answered by the [[Cash flow statement projection]]s.

The pitch of the [[Financial plan]] should include an answer to these two questions and cover other crucial points, such as:

| Crucial point      | Aspects                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Assumptions made   |                           |
| Revenues           | Level<br>Growth<br>Timing |
| Costs              | Nature<br>Level<br>Growth |
| Profitability      | Drivers                   |
| Funding needed?    |                           |
| Cash flow analysis |                           |
