Term sheets are a set of contractual clauses that govern the rights and obligations of both the [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneurs]] and investors.

In order to accommodate for the uncertainty that entrepreneurship brings, [[Contingent contracting]] is practiced.

As this [[Term sheets|Term sheet]] forms the basis of the [[Serie A]] contract, which will also have an impact on future contracts. In this way this contract is **dynamic**.

A TS (term sheet) handles different important subjects:
1. [[#Cash flow rights]]
2. [[#Compensation and employment]]
3. [[#Control rights]]
4. [[#Investor liquidity]]
5. Other clauses
# Cash flow rights
Cash flow rights determine how much of cash flow will be returned to each party (investors and entrepreneurs) on exit.

Cash flow rights in [[Term sheets]] do two things:
- Give incentive to entrepreneurs for high performance
- Give investors *downside protection*
## Convertible stocks
Investors obtain [[Convertible stock]]s, which allows them to choose between:
- a debt-like payoff in case of failure
- Common equity conversion in case of success
## Participating preferred stock
Investors can also combine debt and equity features using [[Participating preferred stock]]s, which also returns them a share of the exit value.
## Preference for preferred stock 
Three reasons why preferred stocks are better:
1. Provide incentives to the founders
2. Screens out weaker projects
3. Align investors' and entrepreneurs' expectations
## Convertible notes
At the [[Seed stage]], [[Convertible note]]s can allow small [[Startup|Start-up]]s to attract low funding amounts from unsophisticated investors.
## Impact on valuation
Giving up ownership for downside protection (viewed from the perspective of the investor) isn't a zero-sum game. Investors can increase their expected exit value by implementing downside protection at the cost of ownership shares.
# Compensation and employment
When the venture is funded, the founders become employees. This implies that they:
- can be fired.
- are subject to [[Non-compete agreement|NCA]]/laws.
- They confer their [[Intellectual property rights|Intellectual property]] to their company. 

Now these ex-founders can be compensated using:
- Salary
- Performance bonuses
- [[Stock option]]s

When using these three forms of compensation, two practices are important for the investors:
- Defer[^1] pay as much as possible to save cash
- Provide incentives for long-term value creation
## Vesting
Most compensation for the founders and early employees comes from future sales of common stock. [[Vesting]] is practiced on founders' stocks.

Stock options for the founders and early employees come from the [[Stock option pool]] of the firm.
# Control rights
A company's charter and by-laws introduce four types of control rights:
1. Shareholders' voting rights
2. [[Board of Directors]]
3. Contractual agreements
4. Informal control

See [[Entrepreneurial corporate governance]].
# Investor liquidity
Different rights stated in the contract can have implication on the liquidity of the investor and the [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneur]]:
- [[Redemption right]]
- [[Tag-along right]]
- [[Drag-along right]]
- [[Registration right]]
