Investment deals consist of two types of elements:
- Hard elements: valuation, term sheet (quantitative measures)
- Soft elements: creating incentives, vision, motivation
# Process
The structuring of a deal consists of five steps:
1. Fundraising / pitching
2. Screening opportunities
3. Syndicating
4. Negotiating
5. Closing

![[Process of investment deal structuring.png]]
## Fundraising / pitching
### Preparation
In order to execute good pitches and negotiations, an entrepreneur has to be prepared. They should have an idea of:
- a strong [[Venture Evaluation Matrix|VEM]] of the venture
- [[Financial plan]]
- Share split between founders
- Possible valuation
### Pre-fundraising engagement with investors
Having early contacts and connections with possible investors before the [[Startup|Start-up]] is in need of funding has an advantage. By building a broad network of potential investors, the [[Startup|Start-up]] will in the future have more options to choose from.

Additionally, early contact and connections give for better trust and interaction during negotiations.
### Disclosure dilemma
The [[Disclosure dilemma]] can be a problem during fundraising for some [[Startup|Start-up]]s.
### Idea value
Valuing an idea can be hard as an idea itself doesn't generate cash flows which could be discounted to a price today. The idea needs to be valued together with the assets needed to generate cash flows.

After this, the question is whether or not the profitability of the idea paired with assets is higher than existing alternatives.
## Screening
After the pitching, investors have to screen all the possible investment opportunities.

A useful tool is the [[MATCH tool]], which assesses the fit between investor and entrepreneur.
## Syndicating
In 50 - 80% of cases, investors don't invest alone. They practice [[Investment syndication]].
## Negotiation
Bargaining theory can be used in negotiations. The [[Nash bargaining solution]] simplifies this complex problem.
## Closing
The closing of the deal often requires agreement or commitment from external parties. F.e. a deal require the hiring of a senior manager. In this case a senior manager has to accept in order for the deal to be completed.
# Living with the deal
Two things are important for the survival of the deal:
1. Trust
2. Long-term commitment

Trust can be achieved by:
- Allowing the other party to take action without direct control
- Allowing the parties to make decisions where contracts can't help
