The [[Venture Evaluation Matrix|VEM]] is a framework that can be used to evaluate a business opportunity by assessing how it can generate value and find it's strengths and weaknesses.

![[VEM components.png]]
# Motivation
The [[Venture Evaluation Matrix|VEM]] has uses for both entrepreneurs as investors.

![[Uses of the VEM.png]]
## Entrepreneur
The [[Venture Evaluation Matrix|VEM]] is useful for entrepreneurs, because:
- Incorporates the entrepreneurial and investor perspectives
- Identify where uncertainties exist
- Expose underlying assumptions
- Evaluate the root of the business model
- Evaluate if the idea is cogent[^1] and convincing
## Investor
The [[Venture Evaluation Matrix|VEM]] is also useful for investors, because:
- Allows for a first screening
- Guides [[Due diligence]]
# Components
## Need
- What exactly is the customer's need?
- How strong is this need?
- What is their [[Willingness to pay]]?
- B2B or B2C?
- Can we learn from [[Early adopters]] (through iterative exploration, see [[Why the lean start-up changes everything by Blank|Blank]])
## Solution
- Does the product solve the customer need?
- How does it compare to alternatives?
- Can the innovation be protected?

Often, the perfect product is found through an iterative product, not from one *Aha!*-moment. This is why the following are crucial:
- [[Product experimentation]]
- [[Design thinking]]
## Team
- Do the founders have the required skills and experience?
- Are they motivated and committed?
- Is the team complementary and cohesive?
(see [[Entrepreneurial teams]])
## Market
We assess how *big* the opportunity is:
- How large is the [[Target market analysis|Target market]]?
- How fast will the [[Target market analysis|Target market]] grow?
- How will adoption happen?

Some important related subjects:
- [[Transformative innovation]] or [[Incremental innovation]]
- [[Market timing]]
- [[Product life cycle]] 
## Competition
- Who are current competitors?
- Who are future competitors?
- What is the nature of the competition?
- How can ventures differentiate?

(see [[Porter's five forces]])

Here, we also have to think about cooperation or competition with similar firms.
## Network
- What is the founding team's reputation?
- What network does the team have access to?
- How does the team create and maintain relations?

Quality of network ties is decided by:
1. Connectedness
2. Diversity
3. Position
## Sales
- How does the product reach the customer?
- [[Marketing strategy]]
- [[Distribution strategy]]
- [[Pricing strategy]]
- [[Revenue model]]
## Production
- Scope
- Partnering strategy
- Efficiency of operations
- Development strategy

Here we decide on the boundaries of the firm and keeping track of progress using milestones.
## Organization
To develop and maintain company leadership we have to think about:
- Evolution of the founders' roles
- [[Corporate Governance]]
- Talent strategy
# Evaluation
![[VEM evaluation of components.png]]
## Horizontal
Using the [[#Components]] above **per row** we can make assessments on the venture's ability to:
1. Create value
2. Scale
3. Grow (and capture value)
## Vertical
Using the [[#Components]] above **per column** we can make assessments on the competitive advantage of the venture on three domains and its ability to:
1. Gain access to customers
2. Set-up and maintain entry barriers
3. Develop key competences

The three columns also give information about the venture's main risks:
1. Market risk
2. Technology risk
3. People risk

[^1]: Logical and clear, in the context of an argument.

The [[Venture Evaluation Matrix|VEM]] can also be used to develop and structure the [[Business plan]], since it also has to do the two following things:
1. Provide a strategic framework for the venture
2. Describe the venture to outside partners
