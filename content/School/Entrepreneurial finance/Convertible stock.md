A convertible stock is a type of [[Preferred stock]], which obtains a yearly *preferred return* (a debt-like fixed dividend), which accumulates the [[Preferred term]] over time:
$$ Preferred \: term (PT) = I + Div $$
$$ PT = I + I*D*T $$
With:
- $I =$ initial investment
- $D=$ started yield
- $T=$ time to exit
- Dividends are compound as $Div=I*D^T$
# Conversion
When cash flow from [[Common stock]] exceeds those from the [[Convertible stock]]s, they will practice their conversion right. The following is required:
$$ F_{INV} * X = PT $$
This gives the following Conversion threshold ($CT$):
$$ CT = \frac{PT}{F_{INV}} $$

# Multiple liquidation preferences
[[Convertible stock]]s may repay the investors capital back multiple times:
$$ PT = M * I + Div $$
Which gives the following critical threshold:
$$ CT_M = \frac{PT}{F_{Inv}} = \frac{(M * I + Div)}{F_{Inv}} > CT $$
