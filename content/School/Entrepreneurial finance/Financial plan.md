A financial plan uses [[Financial projections]] to give insights on the (future) financials of the venture.
# Key questionscomited
A financial plan answers two key questions:
> How financially attractive is the venture?
> *(Financial goals)* 
> What financial resources does the venture need?
> *(Financial means)*

These questions should be answered by the [[Financial projections]].

This requires [[Managerial accounting tools]], which differ from [[Financial accounting tools]] as these look forward instead of on past facts.

In the early stages, a quarterly plan up to 2 years forward should be made.
In the later stages, less frequent but further in the future.
# Motivation
Financial planning is advised for a startup because:
- Predict needed financial resources
- Better external financing negotiation position
- Choose between projects
# Behavioral biases during pitching
Several biases can have an impact on the [[Financial projections]] and pitch of the [[Financial plan]]:
- [[Framing]]
- [[Sunk cost fallacy]]
- [[Over-optimism]]
# Long- and short-term
A financial plan consists of a long-term plan and a short-term plan. These both focus on different aspects of a company and the components of the balance sheet.

![[Balance sheet components.png]]

Besides these eight components, two important metrics should be calculated and compared:
- [[Working capital]]
- [[Working capital requirement]]
## (Medium) Long-term planning
> *Growth planning*

This part assesses:
- Growth planning
- Growth financing
- Financial policy
- Financial stability

![[Long-term growth components.png]]

For a company to grow, an investment in fixed assets is required. For some types of companies (f.e. services), these investments don't have to be large to grow.
### Effects of growth
![[Affected components of the balance sheet in growth.png]]

When a company grows, these three components are affected:

| Component             | How                                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *Inventories*         | When a company grows, companies tend to have too much inventory due to delivery delays.                 |
| *[[Customer credit]]* | $Outputs \uparrow \: \Rightarrow Outgoing \: bills \uparrow \: \Rightarrow Customer \: credit \uparrow$ |
| *[[Supplier credit]]* | $Inputs \uparrow \: \Rightarrow Incomming \: bills \uparrow \: \Rightarrow Supplier \: credit \uparrow$ |
