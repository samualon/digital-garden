Staged financing is the raising of funds of a [[Startup|Start-up]] through successive rounds. They are often named using *Series* + A - B - C. Pre-[[Venture capital|VC]] rounds are called seed rounds.

By creating milestones, defined important event such as an [[Minimum viable product]], the company can determine how much money is needed to proceed to each successive milestone without having to raise the complete sum of funds that the company would need in its lifetime from investors. 
# Benefits
[[Staged financing]] offers benefits to both [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneur]]s as investors.
## Entrepreneur benefits
Staged financing allows the entrepreneur to reduce dilution of his ownership share (see [[#Example]]).
## Investor benefits
- [[Option value of waiting]]
- [[Power of the purse]]
- Allows for termination of investing
# Example
A firm is in need of different amounts of funds for the different stages of the development of the business. The exit value is 200 million with a chance of $0.40*0.50*0.50=0.10=10\%$.

There are three scenarios:

2. Raising 5M upfront and 10M after two years

# Tranching
Tranching is an *intermediary* form of staged financing where the total required investment is divided into tranches (often two). Reaching the successive tranche is conditional on reaching a milestone.

Tranching is beneficial because:
- staging requires negotiation
- staging carries much uncertainty
- tranching is automatic
# Inside and outside investors
Fundings rounds usually include inside, old, and outside, new, investors. These both have different characteristics.

Inside investors have knowledge on the firm but have already exploited their networks and expertise, while outside investors can bring new knowledge and expertise to the firm.

Considerations have to be made for both these types of investors:
- *Deepness of pockets*
- Expertise
- Network
- Signaling effect (what signal does an inside investor give to outside investors if he doesn't add funding in a round?)
## Retention rate
The retention rate measures how much ownership share an investor retains after a funding round:
$$ RR = \frac{F_1}{F_2} $$
With $F_x =$ ownership share at round $x$.
## Term sheet
Inside investors want high valuation of shares to reduce dilution of their shares over rounds, while outside investors want a low valuation to buy cheap shares.

Inside investors can protect themselves from such lower valuations by incorporating certain rights in to the [[Term sheets|Term sheet]]:
- [[Liquidation stack]]
- [[Anti-dilution mechanism]]
- [[Pre-emption right]]
- Right of refusal: allows the company to buy shares from an insider at the same conditions as the offers from an outsider.
