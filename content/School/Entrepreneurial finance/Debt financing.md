Debt financing is the main alternative to equity funding. A lender provides an amount $I$ to the borrower, which the borrower either repays after $T$ periods with interest $r$ or spread out over each period:
$$ D = I*(1+r)^T $$
Or spread out over each period:
$$ D = \sum^T_1 I * r^T + I $$
If the borrower can't repay, this is called defaulting.
# Characteristics
Distinctions can be made between types of debt:
1. [[Installment credit]] or [[Revolving credit]]
2. [[Secured credit]] or [[Unsecured credit]]
3. [[Personal debt]] or [[Corporate debt]]
# Structure of debt contracts
Debt contracts have four elements:
1. Maturity
2. Cost
3. Collateral
4. Covenants
## Maturity
Maturity specifies the date when the credit has to repaid. We talk about:
- Short-term loans if $< 1 \: y$
- Long-term loans if $> 1 \: y$

In case the full payment occurs at maturity, we speak of a [[Bullet loan]]. In case of a [[Revolving credit]], we talk of [[Roll-over]].
## Cost
Debt usually incurs two forms of cost:
- Fees
- Intrest rates
	- Fixed
	- Variable
## Collateral
[[Collateral]] offer lenders protection in case the borrower defaults.
## Covenants
In addition, [[Covenants]] can also protect the lenders from misbehavior of their borrowers.
# Debt versus equity
A common myth is that debt is cheaper than equity. As firms can default on debt, this implies a higher required *interest* rate.

We can compare debt and equity on two dimensions:
1. Transaction costs
2. Incentives
## Transaction costs
Transaction costs incur at three moments:

|            |          Time of the deal          | During the investment |    At repayment     |
| ---------- | :--------------------------------: | :-------------------: | :-----------------: |
| **Debt**   |                Low                 |    [[Tax shield]]     | Cost of defaulting  |
| **Equity** | High (finding investors, pitching) |   No [[Tax shield]]   | Organizing for exit |
## Incentives
Lenders want to ensure repayment, so they prioritize keeping away from [[Bankruptcy]] and slow growth over high growth.

On the other hand, since investors' profits are dependent on the valuation of the firm, they prioritize growth. They are willing to push the firm's growth hard, even if this comes with the risk of [[Bankruptcy]].

# Banks and start-ups
Banks prefer lending to firms with:
- Predictable [[Cash flow]]s
- Stable earnings
- Liquid assets

[[Entrepreneurial firm]]s are a complete opposite. The three metrics that banks use to make lending decisions are unfavorable for [[Startup|Start-up]]s:
1. Probability of default
2. Recovery rate in case of default
3. How high intrest rate can be set
