Bootlegging is the behavior that some [[Corporate entrepreneurship|Intrapreneurs]] show when developing [[Entrepreneurship|Entrepreneurial]] ideas inside of a company. It is characterized by four aspects:
1. It happens secretly
2. It's bottom-up
3. It's non-programmed (not a task of the employee) and thus not officially authorized by management
4. It's for the benefit of the firm
# Why bootlegging occurs
Employees practice bootlegging because:
1. They want to have a strong and more concretely developed idea before presenting it to the company to **increase chances of funding**.
2. It creates less pressure for the intrapreneur.
3. They don't want to be sanctioned for the unauthorized activity.
# Types
Three types of bootleggers exist, each with their own motivation:
1. [[Missionary bootlegger]]
2. [[User bootlegger]]
3. [[Explorer bootlegger]]

Each of [[#Types|these types of bootleggers]] don't want to instantly disclose their ideas, so the firm has to incentives them to do so.

# Research
Researching [[Bootlegging]] is difficult as it happens in secret, but some research has been done:
- Intrapreneurs disclose the bootlegged innovation when it looks reasonable.
- 45% of [[Research and development|R&D]] employees exercise [[Bootlegging]].
- [[Bootlegging]] improves the innovative performance of these employees.
