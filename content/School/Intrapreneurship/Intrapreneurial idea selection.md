The first selection filters out the bad ideas from the good ideas. The second selection is the decision from management whether to fund an idea or not.
# Endorsement
A study on endorsements during idea selection has shown several findings:
- The amount of endorsements increases chances of selection.
- The amount of endorsements **by higher management** increases chances of selection even more.
## Implications
When the company needs ideas in line with the current business and activities, it should support the impact that endorsements from higher management have on selection, as ideas that fit the company are more likely to receive such endorsements.

When the company needs highly innovative ideas, it shouldn't involve higher management in the early selections to mitigate the previous effect.
# Biases
Four forms of biases can impact [[Intrapreneurial idea selection]]:
1. In-group identity
2. Idea format
3. Idea novelty
4. Sequence
## In-group identity
Employees are more likely to prefer ideas that originate from the part of the organization that the employee is active in.

This effect is lower in two situations:
- The selecting employee is part of a large unit of the organization.
- The idea submitter is higher up the corporate hierarchy.
## Idea format
The length of an idea description has an impact on the selection process:
- Long descriptions are more cognitively demanding
- Long descriptions seem too complex
- Short ideas seem superficial[^1]

The optimal length of the idea description is 250 words.
## Idea novelty
The level of novelty of an idea has an impact on selection. Novelty is deemed good by selectors, but too much novelty requires more cognitive thinking, which decreases chance of selection.
## Sequence
When a first project gets a high score, the following project gets a lower score.

This is the result of the [[Law of small numbers]]. *"The second one can't be good too, else that would mean that a big portion of the projects would be good."*
## Mitigation
| Bias                   | Mitigation                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[#In-group identity]] | Anonymize information                                      |
| [[#Idea format]]       | Standardize submissions (with a max length)                |
| [[#Sequence]]          | Use a diverse selection panel (demographic and experience) |
# Randomized idea selection
Using a lottery to select ideas has the advantage of eliminating biases, but good ideas might not get selected.

Typically, selection is fairly easy early-on and later-on. Randomized selection could be used at the middle of selection (medium amound of ideas).
# Metrics
Using the [[McKinsey Three horizons model]], ideas can be categorized in the three horizons H1, H2 and H3.

Since H1 is in the short-term, financial metrics can be estimated accurately and can thus be used for selection.
