# Integrate vs separate
Businesses have two options for this structure:
- Use existing business units
- Create a separate venture

This decision depends on two factors:
- Conflict between old and new business
- Similarities between old and new business

![[Intrapreneurship integration and separation.png]]
## Success factors for separation
Separation requires some other factors to succeed:
- Operational and financial autonomy
- Allow its own culture
- Appoint an internal CEO as head of the new venture (from the existing business)
# Ambidextrous organizations
An [[Ambidextrous organization]] allows companies to both exploit and explore, allowing for [[Corporate entrepreneurship|Intrapreneurship]].

These organizations should internally interact, which can be fostered with [[Spacial ambidexterity]].
# Cyclicality of internal corporate venturing
Corporations often set up a [[Corporate venturing unit]], then shut it down after a few years if they don't notice benefits. This often includes the firing of the employees that gained experience in [[Corporate venturing unit|Corporate venturing]].

After some time, these companies then want to start over again because:
- Prospects of current business in the future are low
- They have slack financial resources

# Nestlé
Nestlé distinguishes renovation and innovation:
- **Renovation**: small incremental changes
- **Innovation**: big changes

[[Nestlé]] created a separate business unit for the [[Corporate entrepreneurship|Intrapreneur]] of the Nespresso machine, because:
- No resistance from his current business unit
- Can get funding from other parts of the organization
- More autonomous, can work how he wants and isn't limited by the way things are usually done.
