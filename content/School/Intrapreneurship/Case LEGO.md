# Case content
	The LEGO Group, founded in 1932 and based in Billund, Denmark, faced significant challenges in the early 2000s due to competition from low-cost imitators and the rise of digital entertainment. To address these threats, LEGO shifted its business model to integrate fan engagement, forge new partnerships, and build new capabilities centered around a customer-centric approach.
	
	Under the leadership of CEO Jørgen Vig Knudstorp, who took over in 2004, LEGO underwent a transformation that focused on reconnecting with its core values and leveraging the passion and creativity of its fan base. This shift was crucial in revitalizing the company, which had experienced years of declining profits and market share.
	
	One of the key strategies was to engage deeply with the Adult Fans of LEGO (AFOL) community. These fans, who were often more knowledgeable than LEGO’s own employees, contributed significantly to product development and innovation. For example, the MINDSTORMS product line, which integrates robotics and computer programming, benefited greatly from fan input. By making the MINDSTORMS firmware open source, LEGO allowed fans to create and share their own programs, fostering a vibrant community of hobbyists and educators.
	
	LEGO also restructured its organization to better align with its new strategy. The company was divided into two main groups: the Markets and Products (M&P) group, which focused on core products, and the Community, Education, and Direct (CED) group, which explored new business opportunities and customer engagement. This structure allowed LEGO to maintain its traditional product lines while also innovating and experimenting with new ideas.
	
	Technological advancements played a crucial role in LEGO’s turnaround. The company leveraged web-based platforms and social media to engage with its fans and gather feedback. Initiatives like LEGO CUUSOO, an open forum where users could propose and support new product ideas, exemplified this approach. Successful ideas from these platforms were developed into actual products, further strengthening the bond between LEGO and its community.
	
	The transformation also included operational efficiencies, such as reducing the number of brick designs and outsourcing distribution to external partners. These measures helped streamline production and reduce costs, contributing to LEGO’s financial recovery.
	
	By embracing a customer-centric approach and leveraging the creativity of its fan base, LEGO not only revitalized its brand but also established a sustainable model for future growth. The company’s success story highlights the importance of engaging with customers and fostering a culture of innovation and collaboration
