[[Market liquidity]] is the degree to which an order can be executed quickly and close to the asset's agreed upon value. In financial markets, this friction is expressed in the form of two prices, the [[Ask price]] and [[Bid price]].
# Definitions
| Term             | Definition                                                                             |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| *Bid*            | Price at which the trader can sell immediately                                         |
| *Ask*            | Price at which the trader can buy immediately                                          |
| *Midquote*       | The average of the ask and bid                                                         |
| *Bid-ask spread* | $Ask - Bid$                                                                            |
| *Depth*          | Number of shares available at a certain ask or bid quote                               |
| *Tick size*      | Minimum price variation to change a quote (prices are discreet, no infinite precision) |
| Dealer           | Liquidity supplier                                                                     |
| Trader           | Liquidity demander                                                                     |
(the minimum Bid-ask spread is the tick size, can't be smaller)
# Measures of liquidity
Both the spread and depth are measures of [[Market liquidity]].

The spread is an inverse measure of liquidity. A high spread means higher liquidity and vise versa. If the spread is 1\$, then you can f.e. buy a share at 30 and sell it maximally at 29. This means the dealer *gains* 1\$, which is incurred for [[Order processing costs]], [[Asymmetric information|Information asymmetry]] and [[Inventory risk]].

A higher depth means a higher liquidity.
