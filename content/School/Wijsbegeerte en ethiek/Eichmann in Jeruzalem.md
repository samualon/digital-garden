Written by [[Hannah Arendt]], the book highlights the banality of the deeds of [[Adolf Eichmann]] during the second world war.
# Eichmann
Eichmann was responsible for the deportation of Jews to destruction camps. However, during his process in Israel, the man has the appearance of being a second-class person. In her book she highlights how people alike Eichmann could have led to such evil and radical deeds.
# The banality of evil
Arendt states that evil banal, enigmatic. The holocaust had taken place because the nazi's *atomized* the entire process in a bureaucratic way. Hitler was the sole responsible for the evil, all others were simply small parts of the plan who executed their orders (willingly or not).

This evil wasn't radical, but a sum of thousands of separate activities.
