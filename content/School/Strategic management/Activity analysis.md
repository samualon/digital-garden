In order to identify the drivers of [[competitive advantage]] and execute an [[Activity analysis]], the company needs to be broken down into activities (see [[CR08 Strategy and the business landscape#Activity analysis]]). 

Several steps have to be undertaken:
1. Identify firm activities.
2. Identify costs of activities and compare to competitors (why are they different?).
3. Analyze how each activity contributes to the [[Willingness to pay|WTP]] and why;
4. Consider strategy changes to increase wedge between costs and [[Willingness to pay|WTP]].
# Identify firm activities
The [[Value chain]] can be used to identify all activities of the company. A more elaborate form of the [[Value chain]] is [[Porter's value chain]].
- Estimate the importance of each activity for sustaining the competitive advantage.
- Identify *scale-scope-learning advantages*.
- Identify **interdependence** and **complementarity** of activities.
- Identify coordination costs.

The main question to be asked is: *"Do all activities reinforce our competitive advantage and are they coherent with our scope?*".

Some companies will focus on the *head* of the [[Value chain]], others on the tail.
# Identify costs / cost drivers
As managers have laid out all activities of the company, they then have to define all the cost [[Cost driver]]s of these activities.
## Tips
- A cost driver should be modeled only if it is likely to vary across the competitors or in terms of the strategic options that will be considered.
- As these steps rely on assumptions, a [[Sensitivity analysis]] is necessary.
- Difference in available resources can be a cost driver.
# Analyze WTP
Activities not only impact costs, but also the [[Willingness to pay|WTP]] of customers and thus the [[Economic value added]] and profitability.

[[Willingness to pay|WTP]] can be impacted through all activities, but activities relating to the characteristics of the product have the most impact.

To *calculate* [[Willingness to pay|WTP]], a method of four steps can be used:
1. Define *real* buyers of the product/service
2. Pinpoint what these buyers want
3. Find how successful the firm is at meeting these customer expectations
4. Relate the successfulness of step 3. to the relevant activities

The [[Value curve]] is a useful way of graphically representing the [[Willingness to pay|WTP]] of customers.
# Consider strategy changes to increase wedge
- Consider customers' reactions to the strategic changes.
- Consider unobserved customer segments.
- Evaluate the possible impact of all activities, not only those related to the product/service's characteristics.
- In rapidly developing markets, [[Early adopters]] can be a proxy of what the broader public wants.
- Changing the scope of targeted customers can be the most impactful strategic decision.
