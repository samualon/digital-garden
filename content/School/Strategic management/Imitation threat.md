Imitation threat is the threat of competitors imitating the processes and products you use to create [[Economic value added|Added economic value]].
# Mitigation
Six barriers allow for a mitigation of this threat:
- Private information
- Switching costs
- Size economies
- Threat of retaliation
- Imitation lags
- Upgrading
## Private information
By making superior information private, you can make it costly for competitors to imitate, which mitigates the incentive of copying.
## Switching costs
By introducing switching costs and strengthening relationships with suppliers, customers, etc. a firm is able to make it harder for competitors to execute similar processes.
## Size economies
Three size economies can work as a mitigation of imitation:
1. [[Economies of scale]]
2. [[Economies of scope]]
3. [[Economies from learning by doing]]
## Threat of retaliation
By using the [[Competitive advantage]] and resource reserves, the firm can put pressure on competitors not to imitate.
## Imitation lags
Typically, imitating takes a lot of time. As a rule of thumb, [[Research and development]] of a new product takes four to six years.
## Upgrading
By continuously increasing the firms [[Economic value added|Added economic value]] and driving a larger wedge between [[Willingness to pay|WTP]] and costs, the firm becomes a moving target for competitors. This makes it harder to keep up with, even if they try to imitate.
