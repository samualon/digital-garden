Substitution, more specifically research substitution, is a threat which involves displacing scarce resources of the [[Incumbent]], instead of duplicating (like [[Imitation threat|imitaiton]]).
# Mitigation
Seven barriers exist for substitution:
1. Not responding
2. Migrating and harvesting
3. Defending
4. Straddling
5. Switching
6. Recombining
7. [[Leapfrogging]]
## Migrating and harvesting
Migrating involves relocating resources to uses that are less susceptive to substitution.

Harvesting involves *milking* existing resources instead of rebuilding and maintaining them.
## Defending
This involves increasing the wedge between [[Willingness to pay|WTP]] and costs.
## Straddling
Straddling involves both relying on the firm's previous superior resources and the new substituting resources.
## Switching
This is a more drastic reaction than [[#Straddling]] and involves completely switching to the substitute.
## Recombining
Combining the old ways and resources with the new possibilities, which works better than [[#Switching]].
## Leapfrogging
[[Leapfrogging]] is the most radical move of a company to respond to substitution.
