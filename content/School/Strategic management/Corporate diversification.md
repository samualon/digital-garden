Research has shown that in the US (using the [[S&P 500]] as a sample), conglomerates (or heavily diversified companies) generate less returns than focused companies. This is termed as the [[Diversification discount]].

| Type              | Returns |
| ----------------- | ------- |
| Conglomerates     | 7.5%    |
| Focused companies | 11.8%   |
Are there any reasons then for companies to diversify instead of focus?
# Emerging markets
In emerging markets, such as South Korea and India, the biggest companies are conglomerates with respective growth rates of 11% and 23%.

The reason for this is the fact that institutions in emerging markets heavily differ from established markets.
## Institutions and strategy
The US and India as an example show how companies exist in different environments, which make it such that conglomeration is a better strategy in emerging markets.

| Dimension              | US                        | India                            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| [[Capital markets]]    | Equity-focused            | Underdeveloped,<br>Not monitored |
| Labor markets          | Many business schools     | Few business schools             |
| Product markets        | Many laws and information | Few laws and information         |
| Government regulations | Low corruption            | High corruption                  |
| Contract enforcement   | Predictable               | Unpredictable                    |
Emerging markets such as India are often called [[Institutional voids]].
