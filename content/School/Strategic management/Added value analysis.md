# History
In the mid-1990s, Brandenburger and Stuart created a framework to maximize the gap between costs and product value. They analyzed suppliers, competitors, and buyers, defining total value as the difference between customer willingness to pay and supplier costs.

They introduced "added value" to measure each participant's contribution to this total value, helping to optimize value creation and distribution in business.

Unattractive industries' added values are low. The lack of added value for the industry is a whole makes a firm-specific competitive advantage more impacting.

Attractive industries' added values are high. The competitive advantage of the firms is less impacting than the high added values that they can profit from in the industry.
