Active listening is a psychological method created by [[Carl Rogers]]. His method of listening to his patients helped them feel better and solve their own problems.

[[Active listening]] can be used in many aspects of life and can foster more deep and meaningful conversations with people while also making them feel better.
# Core
in a conversation, typically two parts exist:
- The content of the message
- The emotion behind it

A crucial part of [[Active listening]] is understanding either of these, or even both.
# Method
These four tools can improve your listening ability:
- [[#Be on the same page]]
- [[#Minimal encourages]]
- [[#Showing empathy]]
- [[#Become interested]]
## Be on the same page
By paraphrasing and summarizing in a natural casual way that the other person would agree with. It helps making sure that you're both on the same page.
## Minimal encourages
Using [[Minimal encourages]], you can encourage the other person to express themselves more fully. 

This also allows you time to process what was said to formulate a thoughtful response.
## Showing empathy
By practicing [[Emotion labeling]], you can increase the emotional connection. By using it naturally and casually, the person will feel heard.
## Become interested
Instead of trying to make people interested in you, you should focus on [[Active listening]] and become genuinely interested in them. 

By practicing to see the world through the eyes of the other speaker, this will shape an attitude which allows to be truly genuine interested in the speaker.
# Tips
Some other tips that can improve your [[Active listening]] are:
- Encouraging elaboration through open questions, not solely [[Minimal encourages]].
- Summarize key points to reinforce the speaker's thoughts and ensuring clarity. It can also show that you understand the whole picture.
- Offering supportive statements
